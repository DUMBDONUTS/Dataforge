What if an AI could reason without writing every step?

Chain-of-thought forces a model to narrate its reasoning as tokens. BDH-CQ takes a different path: iterative computation in a recurrent latent state, decoding only candidate answers. This lab lets you test both on the same problem.

      THE MECHANISM

      Where does the reasoning happen?

      CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT

      problem→tokens→more tokens→answer

      Each reasoning step becomes new text the model reads on the next step. Inspectable, but serial and token-costly.

      BDH-CQ LATENT REASONING

      problem→state ↻ state ↻ state→answer

      Iterative refinement in a fixed-size recurrent state. Only candidate answers decoded. Cheaper compute, zero CoT tokens.

      Why it can be cheaper

      CoT adds generated tokens per step. Recurrent systems allocate extra work as internal passes without growing a written trace.

      Why it is harder to trust

      You cannot read a latent vector as a sentence. Evaluation and verification must replace inspection of written rationale.

      What this does not claim

      Latent reasoning is not automatically more accurate, universally cheaper, or a replacement for auditable reasoning. Results depend on architecture and task.

      CASE STUDY · PATHWAY BDH-CQ

      BDH-CQ puts latent reasoning into a reported system.

      Pathway describes BDH-CQ as a 150M-parameter system that learns from demonstrations by updating recurrent latent state at inference. It decodes candidate answers rather than chain-of-thought, with no per-task parameter updates.
